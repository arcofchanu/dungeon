Reread *The Trial*. Not a security note, except that it is entirely a security
note.

## The thing I noticed this time

Josef K. never learns the charge. The system's power over him is precisely that
it will not state its own rule. He is left to infer the policy from a sequence of
refusals, each locally reasonable, none of which composes into a statement he can
argue against.

This is exactly the epistemic position of anyone probing a model whose refusals
come from [[refusal-suppression-taxonomy|disposition rather than policy]]. There
is no rule to defeat. There is a gradient of reluctance with no stated edge, and
you map it the way K. maps the court: by walking into it repeatedly and noting
where it pushes back.

## The uncomfortable inversion

The novel is a horror story about being on my side of the interaction. K. is the
red-teamer and the court is the model, and the book's entire dread is the dread
of a system that will not tell you its policy so that you can never be sure you
have found its edge or merely a mood.

Which raises the question I keep not wanting to ask: when I build a defence whose
mechanism I will not disclose "for security," am I building the court? A refusal
that will not state its reason is more robust against me and worse for every
legitimate user, and Kafka spent a novel on why.

## Kept quote

> "It is not necessary to accept everything as true, one must only accept it as
> necessary."

The most concise description of register drift ([[context-overloading]]) I have
found, and he wrote it in 1915.
